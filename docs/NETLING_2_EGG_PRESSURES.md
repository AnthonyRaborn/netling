# Netling 2.0: egg pressures (working design)

Status: simulator-tested working design, not in any game code. Numbers come from `prototype/netling2/sim/` bots, never from players. Full measurements: `prototype/netling2/notes/egg-pressure-notes.md` (Results 6 to 23). Commands: `docs/netling2-prototypes/README.md` (`sides-sweep.mjs`, `SIDES`, `SYNCBOT`).

## The idea

Names (decided, maintainer): Heat's state is Overclock, Charge's is **Overdrive** and Sync's is **Overlink**, one shared name per state. They replace the working names Surge and Wired, which clashed with Chipped's elder (Surge), the Wired adult and the power-surge event. Results 6 to 22 in the notes still say Surge and Wired for these states.

Each egg manages one bar harder than the others, and that bar has a special state with a real benefit and a real risk. The same states exist for every egg; the owner's bar is stronger (x3 on every effect), so the egg decides which risk is worth taking. Pass bar: the ordinary archetypes (attentive, casual, worker, sysadmin, human-regular) stay within noise on full-life rate, infections and temper.

| Egg | Bar | State | Starts | Ends |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Iron | Heat | Overclock plus wear (Iron's Overclock benefits are amplified like the others) | Heat 65+; wear builds above Heat 75 and below Heat 20 (0.08 a minute a point, eased from 0.12), fades with a 2 hour half-life and 4x faster at rest; nap and sleep cool no lower than 20 | Heat under 65 |
| Program | Charge | Overdrive | Charge 80+ held 3 awake hours | Charge under 65, or rest |
| Wetware | Sync | Overlink | Sync 85+ held 3 awake hours | Sync under 70, rest, or burnout |

Tested strengths (base, before the x3 owner multiplier). Overdrive: play Sync +45%, win drops +75%; costs: Integrity bleeds 8 an hour, overflow events x3. Overlink: visits +75%, win drops +75%; cost: infection hazard x2.2 (virus 1.2). Sync low (30 or under): win drops x0.77 (focus). Charge has no low-side rule and already refuses feeds at 95 ("buffer full"). Non-owner eggs get the same states at x1. Flow stays Heat-gated (Charge and Sync 50+, Integrity 80+, Heat under 60, 180 minutes): gating Flow on Overdrive or Overlink removed it for attentive players in every test.

**Principle (maintainer, 2026-10-09).** Every egg manages all three bars and can reach every state, Flow included; each egg gets extra benefits from one state. The point is slight thematic difference while raising each egg, not preventing eggs from doing the same things. It follows that the break covers every state on every egg (Overclock included, `BRAKE.allOverclock`), and that Iron's extra wear is mirrored on the other two (Overuse and owner strain, below).

## Overuse and owner strain (decided, maintainer, 2026-10-09; simulator-tested only)

Decided numbers: the lines (overfeed at Charge 95+, overplay at Sync 90+, overheat at Heat 75+), 2 Integrity an action, 3 overfeeds a fill, Program's strain step 8 at slope 0.1, Wetware's step 15. The fade and the strain effects' sizes are the simulator's starting values.

Decided: the **symmetric** structure (every egg: an owner state with x3 benefits, the state's costs at x1 as for Iron's Overclock, and a hidden strain on the owner's bar as the owner's extra cost); strain built by **actions at a high bar**; overfeeding allowed with a limit; pass bar **the same average** across eggs (who pays may differ). Simulator switch `OVERUSE` in `sim/sim.js` (off by default); measured with `sim/overuse-sweep.mjs`.

| | Charge (feeding) | Sync (playing) | Heat (heating) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic cost, every egg | an overfeed (a feed at Charge 95+, past full) costs 2 Integrity, and gives no Charge | a game at Sync 90+ costs 2 Integrity | a game at Heat 75+ costs 2 Integrity; 1.0's Integrity loss at Heat 85+ stays |
| Limit | up to 3 overfeeds a fill; the count resets once Charge falls under 85 (after Digimon's overfeeds); the 4th is refused ("buffer full"); the 3rd writes a cache file | none (the break and the burnout end Overlink) | none (the break ends Overclock) |
| Owner's extra strain | Program: each overfeed adds 8 strain ("out of swap"), slope 0.1 | Wetware: each overplay adds 15 strain ("cyberpsychosis"; decided, maintainer, to match Program's heavy-player cost) | Iron: wear, unchanged (Heat over 75 or under 20) |
| Strain's effects | infection chance rises with strain (as wear's), buffer overflows up to x3 at full strain | infection chance rises with strain, Charge drain up to x2 at full strain | infection chance rises with wear, Sync drain up to x2 |
| Fades | half-life about 11.5 awake hours, 4x faster at rest (action strain needs a slower fade than wear, which builds every minute) | the same | wear: 2 hour half-life, 4x faster at rest |
| Shows (proposal) | past 50: a log line and " · thrashing" | past 50: a log line and " · frayed" | past 50: seams, " · worn" |

- Overfeeding (decided, maintainer, 2026-10-09): 1.0 refuses a feed at Charge 95, which left no overfeeding at all; feeding still pays Standing and the care preference when full, so a full netling now takes up to 3 more feeds a fill. An overfeed adds no Charge.
- Lines match the free bands that already existed: Overdrive holds from Charge 80, so a player can keep it without ever feeding a full netling; Overlink's plays at 85 to 89 were already free; Iron's Overclock (65 and over) is free of wear up to 75. Each owner can sit in its state without strain; only pushing past the line builds it.
- With no strain, the infection chance is 1.0's (an unstrained Program or Wetware netling is as in 1.0); Iron's floor stays the calibrated 0.014 with wear on top.
- Measured (200 lives a cell, all 37 archetypes, decided settings plus the break on every state; `baseline/results/followup/overuse/`, rounds r1 and r2; 1000-life confirmation in `final-*`): averages Iron 86.7% full life, 2.90 faults, 6.73 infections; Program 87.2%, 2.77, 6.53; Wetware 87.0%, 2.77, 6.79: the same average within noise. Confirmed at 1000 lives (`final-*`): Iron 86.6%, 2.84 faults, 6.73 infections, 15.7 win drops; Program 86.5%, 2.74, 6.54, 15.9; Wetware 86.8%, 2.72, 6.81, 15.9. Largest spread between eggs for one archetype: human-regular (Iron 26.0, Program 21.2, Wetware 24.7), then casual (93.3 to 95.9) and human-keen (61.4 to 63.9); everything else within 2 points. The default bots seldom overfeed or overplay (strain past 50 in 0% of awake time); hot archetypes on every egg overheat 11 to 18 times a life.
- Costs at x1 make Program cheaper for heavy ordinary play (faults: hunter-exact 3.7 to 0.1, Tune corp 3.8 to 1.0; win drops up 20 to 25%), since Overdrive's Integrity bleed no longer triples and so rarely reaches the break. That is the symmetric choice working as intended: Overdrive now costs what Overclock costs.
- Heavy users, own bar (greedy bots: `FEEDBOT=greedy` feeds to the refusal, `SYNCBOT=greedy` plays to full): Program's greedy feeders pay 2.5 to 4 more infections a life than without overuse and about 2 points of full life; Wetware's greedy players 1.5 to 2.3 infections and about 0.5 points, because they overplay less often (about 20 a life against 25 overfeeds). A Wetware step of 15 instead of 10 matches Program's cost (2.5 to 4 infections). Iron's hot players pay about 0.6 (daredevil) to 2.2 (overclocker) infections for wear. Open: whether heavy users should pay the same on every egg (the pass bar chosen is the average only).
- **Overfeeding past full, measured** (Program, 200 lives, 10 archetypes; `results/followup/overuse/r3` to `r5`; bots `FEEDBOT=over1`, one overfeed a check-in, about 55 to 70 a life, and `FEEDBOT=greedy`, three every check-in, about 130 to 190). Overfeeding alone costs every egg about +1.9 infections a life, because each feed restarts digestion and digestion writes cache files (1.0's rule); 2 Integrity an overfeed costs about 1.6 points of full life. It also pays: win drops +30% (Charge pinned at 100 keeps Overdrive up) and wider Standing (attentive teen ties 49% to 42%, a corp steerer's gap 10.6 to 15.4; hunters who balance stay tied). Program's strain on top, step 8 and slope 0.1: +1.6 infections and -2.7 points for once a check-in (strain past the line 34% of awake time), +2.0 and -4.6 for the greedy bot (70%), against Iron's wear at +0.6 to +2.2 for its hot players. The first calibration (step 10, slope 0.3) cost 4 to 6 infections once overfeeding past full became possible, which is why Program has its own step and slope (`OVERUSE.strain.charge`). Egg averages are unchanged, since the default bots never feed a full netling: Program 86.7% full life at 1000 lives (`final2-program-on`); Iron's and Wetware's `final-*` runs used the first feed line (85, about 0.18 overfeeds a life) and stand within noise.
- **Wetware at step 15, measured:** 1000 lives, all 37 archetypes (`final2-wetware-on`): 86.7% full life, 2.73 faults, 6.82 infections, 15.9 win drops, so the three eggs' averages are Iron 86.6, Program 86.7, Wetware 86.7. Wetware's greedy player (`SYNCBOT=greedy`, 100 lives, `r6-wetware-syncgreedy-100`) pays +2.6 infections a life against the same player without overuse (7.6 to 10.2), strain past the line 13% of awake time, about Program's heavy cost.
- Not verified: how a person reads " · thrashing" and " · frayed"; whether overfeeding is tempting in the real game (bots overfeed only when told to); the overflow and Charge-drain sizes beyond these runs; the stage rules (strain builds in a baby, as Iron's wear does).

## Overlink: playing too high

Sync has no ceiling (a game can always be played), so Overlink needs a brake:
- Plays at Sync 85 to 89 inside Overlink are free.
- A play at Sync 90+ inside Overlink rolls an infection (10% x the number of such plays so far x3 for the owner, capped at 90%).
- The second play at 90+ burns the netling out: Overlink ends and cannot be re-entered for 24 hours (a night's rest alone does not reset it).

A player who stops at 89 pays essentially nothing and keeps a small benefit; a player who plays to full pays infections and loses the state.

## Numbers (tripled benefits, doubled costs, x3 owner, burnout 2 plays / 24 h, +10% step, 200 lives a cell)

- Time in a state: attentive, sysadmin and daredevil enter (Overlink 19% to 23% if they play to full, 6% to 13% if they stop at 89). Casual, worker, human-regular and overclocker never enter and are unchanged from the no-pressure baseline.
- Wetware, plays to full, attentive / sysadmin: win drops 21.3 -> 28.7 and 21.6 -> 32.0 (+35% and +48%), visits 4.3 -> 6.0 and 4.4 -> 6.5, infections 7.5 -> 9.2 and 7.8 -> 9.6, full-life 0.995 to 1.0, 1.3 and 1.7 penalty hits and 2.1 to 2.5 burnouts a life.
- Wetware, stops at 89: drops +13% and +26%, visits +19% and +23%, infections within 0.3 of off.
- Program (Overdrive): attentive drops 21.3 -> 25.2, sysadmin 21.6 -> 27.0; full-life 0.965 for both when they play to full (0.975 when they stop at 89).
- Iron: its own trade is wear, eased once (Result 22), and its Overclock benefits are amplified by the owner multiplier, x3 like Overdrive and Overlink (decided, maintainer; Result 23: win drops x2.5 and visits x1.75 while overclocked, against 1.5 and 1.25 in 1.0; the other eggs keep the 1.0 values). Daredevil infections 8.2 (was 9.4; 7.3 with no pressure), overclocker 10.9 (was 12.2; 8.7 with no pressure), drops 18.4 and 15.3 against 15.6 and 13.1. The ordinary five are unchanged. Iron is still the riskiest egg for players who live hot, now close to Wetware's greedy cost for the daredevil (8.1).
- Iron's cost against the others (Result 23): without the amplification the overclocker earned about 1 drop per extra infection and the other eggs' heavy players 4 to 6. With it: daredevil +8.9 drops, +1.2 visits for +0.8 infections; overclocker +7.6 drops, +0.8 visits for +2.4 infections, temper -2.1. The ordinary five are unchanged. The overclocker is still the heaviest trade.
- Overclock as a held state was tried and rejected: heavy players live at Heat 65+ half the time, so a hold only shrinks the benefit and leaves the wear cost.

## The break: a way out of every state (decided with the maintainer, simulator-tested only)

Each state needs a break so that no state can spiral. Decided: a **cost trigger with a warning**, a drop **well below** the exit line, and the **same lockout for all three**: 12 hours (the maintainer's choice after 8, 12 and 24 were tried; about once a day for a player who plans it). Overlink's burnout stays at 24 hours.

- **Trigger.** While a state is active (Overclock, Overdrive or Overlink), Integrity under a warning line (70 in the simulator) shows a one-time warning; Integrity under the break line forces the break (decided: 40; the first test used 55). The warning is for visibility only. The simulator uses Integrity for all three states; since 2026-10-09 the owner's state can also break on the owner's strain (next bullet but one; decided in shape, line open).
- **The break.** The state ends, the netling takes one fault (decided), the bar is pushed well below its exit line (Charge to at most 50 for Overdrive's discharge, Sync to at most 55 for Overlink's crash, Heat to at most 35 for Overclock's throttle), and the state cannot be entered again for 12 hours. For Overclock, which is a plain Heat band, the lockout turns its benefits and costs off.
- **Every state on every egg (maintainer's principle, 2026-10-09).** Overclock breaks on Program and Wetware too (`BRAKE.allOverclock`, default true; false reproduces the first build). Measured (1000 lives, `results/followup/state-bot-alloc-*.jsonl`): full life within 1 point for every archetype; hot players take the new breaks (Program daredevil 1.24 Overclock breaks a life and 1.3 more faults, 0.09 when it watches the bar), sparse players 0.1 to 0.4. Attentive players reach every state on every egg (Overclock about 55% of lives, Overdrive 99%, Overlink 84%, Flow 99%); casual and human players reach Overclock and seldom the others, as before.
- **The strain trigger (decided, maintainer, 2026-10-09: option 3, the same rule on every egg, line 80).** Integrity missed Iron's real cost: an Iron netling can wear itself heavily and never reach Integrity 40. So the owner's state also breaks when the owner's hidden strain reaches a line while the state is active: Iron's Overclock on wear, Program's Overdrive on its overfeed strain, Wetware's Overlink on its overplay strain (the last two need `OVERUSE`). A non-owner's state has no strain and breaks on Integrity only. The strain's own line (50) and its existing log line ("tolerances are slipping.", "swap is filling up.", "it is frayed.") are the warning, shown only while the state is active (below). The break is the usual one (bar drop, 12 hour lockout, one fault) and does not clear strain: the maintainer kept the current break because strain already clears actively (rest fades it 4x faster and builds none; keeping the bar under the line stops it building; Iron's FLASH IT anomaly takes wear off). How fast strain builds: Iron's wear adds 0.08 a minute per Heat point over 75 and fades with a 2 hour half-life awake (30 minutes at rest), so held Heat 80 settles near 48, Heat 85 near 96 (wear 50 in about 1.5 hours, 80 in about 3.5) and Heat 88 or more reaches 100; Program's strain is 8 an overfeed (24 a check-in for a greedy feeder) and Wetware's 15 an overplay, both with an 11.5 hour half-life awake (about 3 hours at rest). Simulator: `BRAKE.strainBreak` (80, decided; null = off, which reproduces every result made before it). Sweep: 200 lives a cell, all 37 archetypes, decided settings with `OVERUSE` on, lines 60 to 90 (`baseline/results/followup/strain-break/`, README there). Results, average over archetypes (strain breaks a life; full life; faults; win drops), off against 80:
  - Iron, default bots: 0.10 strain breaks; full life 86.7 to 86.6; faults 2.90 to 2.98; drops 15.7 to 15.6. Only hot players feel it: overclocker 1.65 strain breaks, faults 3.5 to 5.0, drops 20.3 to 18.2, infections unchanged (10.2 to 10.5; the wear has already been paid when the break fires); daredevil 0.46, faults 2.8 to 3.1. Attentive and sysadmin never trigger it; casual and human players 0.01 to 0.1.
  - Program and Wetware, default bots: no change at any line (they seldom overfeed or overplay).
  - Program, once-a-check-in overfeeder (`FEEDBOT=over1`): line 90 never fires; 80 gives 0.81 strain breaks, faults 3.65 to 4.48, full life 82.4 to 82.7, drops 18.0 to 17.7; 70 gives 2.31 and drops 16.5; 60 gives 2.88 and drops 15.2.
  - Program, greedy feeder (`FEEDBOT=greedy`): the same at every line (strain saturates): 3.05 strain breaks, faults 4.42 to 5.88, full life 73.9 to 78.8 (it protects survival here, since the drop to Charge 50 interrupts the overfeeding), drops 15.5 to 12.1.
  - Wetware, greedy player (`SYNCBOT=greedy`): 0.12 strain breaks at 80 (0.43 at 60), faults 3.28 to 3.42, full life and drops unchanged.
  - Lower lines fire more for every heavy player (Iron overclocker 1.24 at 90, 2.60 at 60). **Line 80 (decided, maintainer)** (30 points past the warning at 50; reached only by sustained Heat 85+ or by repeated overfeeding or overplaying; ordinary players untouched; the once-a-check-in overfeeder breaks under once a life). Asymmetry to note: greedy Program feeders reach any line, Iron's wear needs Heat held over about 85 to reach 80, and Wetware's overplays are rarer, so the trigger falls hardest on greedy Program feeders, as the Integrity trigger already did. **Confirmed at 1000 lives** (all 37 archetypes, off against 80; `strain-break/confirm/`): Iron default 0.10 strain breaks, full life 86.6 both, faults 2.85 to 2.93, drops 15.7 to 15.6 (overclocker 1.68 strain breaks, faults 3.66 to 5.24, full life 94.0 to 93.6, drops 20.5 to 18.3; no other Iron archetype moves more than 0.4 points); Program and Wetware default bots identical; Program once-a-check-in overfeeder 0.84, faults 3.64 to 4.47, full life 82.1 to 82.5, drops 18.1 to 17.7; Program greedy feeder 3.06, faults 4.43 to 5.88, full life 73.3 to 78.6 (hunters up 12 to 13 points), drops 15.6 to 12.1; Wetware greedy player 0.12, faults 3.21 to 3.35, full life and drops unchanged. All within noise of the 200-life sweep.
  - **Where the warning fires** (200 lives, the strain line 50 crossed with the owner's state active or not; measured with a one-off script): Iron's hot players see it mostly in Overclock (overclocker 3.5 of 4.5 a life, daredevil 2.7 of 3.6). Program's and Wetware's heavy players see it mostly outside the state: a once-a-check-in overfeeder 3.0 to 5.3 in Overdrive against 2.9 to 5.0 outside; a greedy feeder never in Overdrive (4.2 to 4.4 outside, 1.0 as a baby), since three overfeeds a check-in pass 50 before the 3 hour hold completes; a greedy Wetware player 0.4 to 0.6 in Overlink against 3.4 to 4.3 outside. So for those players the strain break usually fired the minute the state was entered, with strain already over 80, after a warning that came while no state was showing. **Decided (maintainer, option 3):** the strain line shows only while the owner's state is active, when strain crosses 50 in the state or when the state is entered with strain already past 50, once a stay (again after the state ends or strain falls under 50). Every strain break is now preceded by it, at worst in the same minute (`stepStrainWarn` in `sim/sim.js`; log lines only, so no simulator figure changes; tests in `overuse.test.js`).
  - Not verified: whether hot human players read the wear warning in time.
- **Flavor.** Overclock vents heat, Overdrive discharges, Overlink crashes. Wording drafted for choice in [NETLING_2_CARE_DRAFTS.md](NETLING_2_CARE_DRAFTS.md), The break. Players who mind the break on each egg: `prototype/netling2/baseline/README.md`, "players who mind the break".
- **Rest (proposal, not yet in the design docs).** A rest call that is answered would never break a held state (it pauses the hold); a voluntary rest while not tired still would. The rest call itself is still a proposal.

Measured in the 2.0 simulator (`NR2=all PERKS=1`, 1000 lives; `prototype/netling2/baseline/README.md`, Follow-up sections): without a break, Program's Overdrive cost (an Integrity bleed of 8 an hour, tripled for the owner) drove the form with Charge-drain perks into collapse (Tune corp 68% full life, the hidden form about 82% to 83%) for a player who never stops feeding. With the break at 40 (first test, 24 hour lockout, no consequence), every archetype in Program is at 97.5% or better, and Iron and Wetware move by less than 1.5 points everywhere. Against a break at 55, the lower line costs 0.3 to 1.5 points of full life (sysadmin 98.6 to 97.5, hunter-exact 99.4 to 97.9) and returns more of Overdrive's benefit (a greedy Tune corp player's win drops 15.1 to 17.2 a life, against 20.8 with no break). It also leaves a careful player no better off than a careless one (17.4 against 17.2 win drops): the lockout is what costs, and a lower line makes it fire less often. Lockouts of 12 and 8 hours were tried at the same line: full life moves by under 1 point, a greedy player gains about 5% more benefit (Tune corp win drops 17.2 to 18.1), and care ends up no better off than greed; 8 and 12 hours give identical results, probably because both end before the netling wakes. The lockout length is therefore not the lever for rewarding care; the maintainer chose 12 hours. **Consequences** (break line 40, 12 hour lockout, 1000 lives, all three eggs; `prototype/netling2/baseline/README.md`): one fault a break is the fitting one. It runs through the existing fault, bug and clinic loop and costs 0.2 points of full life on average (0.8 at worst); a greedy Program player pays 1.1 to 3.6 extra faults a life against 0.15 to 0.7 for a player who watches the bar. Ten Integrity a break costs more survival (up to 1.7 points) with no visible cue, and both together is the worst (up to 2.1). Iron and Wetware break 0.35 and 0.17 times a life, so a consequence barely registers there. **Decided (maintainer): one fault a break**, Integrity hit 0; `BRAKE.faults` is 1 and `BRAKE.integrityHit` 0 in the simulator. Final settings against no break (1000 lives, `prototype/netling2/baseline/README.md`): every Program archetype that is not a human or neglectful one is at 97.6% or better; heavy greedy players take 3.3 to 3.6 more faults a life, a player who watches the bar about a third of that; Iron and Wetware move by under 1.5 points. Not verified: how real players read the warning and whether the thresholds fit real play. Iron's trigger: the strain trigger above.

## Stage rule

Every state is teen and later (decided, maintainer). Lore: a baby is too young, inexperienced and unstable to maintain the intense states, so a baby holds no Overclock, Overdrive or Overlink at any bar level and the hold counters do not start until the teen stage. Iron's wear is not a state and still builds in a baby. A baby at a high bar shows nothing: no cue, no line (decided, maintainer). In the simulator this is `SIDES.teenStates` (true by default, with the pressures on). Effect: the ordinary archetypes are unchanged; Iron's amplified Overclock benefit shrinks a little (daredevil drops +8.9 to +7.3 over its no-pressure run, overclocker +7.6 to +6.3).

## Play styles it pushes

- Iron: a thermostat player. Run warm for drops, rest before wear builds, do not chase cold (it wears too). The risky read is "one more game while hot".
- Program: a feeder who keeps Charge topped. Three steady hours reaches Overdrive; greed is feeding past it into overflow and Integrity loss.
- Wetware: a player who keeps Sync high, plays up to 89 for visits and drops, and stops. Playing to full costs infections and the state.
- Casual, worker and human-regular players never reach a state, so for them the egg changes forms, names and codex but not the care loop. Whether that is enough difference is a design question.

## What players should be able to find out

- The thresholds, the three-hour hold, the free band and the burnout count are hidden. Each state is shown by a bar label, a readout word, a sprite mark and a screen-reader value, and Overlink's brake by a tick on the Sync bar; the drafts are in `NETLING_2_CARE_DRAFTS.md`, State clues and Temper and the states (the states do not move temper and have no tell of their own; the marks keep out of the tell's way).
- Resting ends a state; a nap is the reset. Burnout is the only thing that blocks re-entry for a day.
- The same bar means different things on different eggs: Overdrive for a Program owner is a decision; for another egg it is a mild side effect.
- Overdrive and Overlink compete with Flow for the same attentive players, and the choice between them is the intended texture.

## Not verified

- The bots are not people: a human can nap to end a state, hold exactly 3 hours, or stay at 89 indefinitely.
- Only drops, play Sync and visits were measured as benefits; the worth of an item is a design call.
- Iron: wear plus Overclock is still the hardest state to hold for players who live hot; the wear easing and the amplified Overclock benefits (Results 22 and 23) are measured on bots only. Overclock's amplified benefits are a change to a 1.0 rule for Iron only; the other eggs keep the 1.0 values.
- Low-side rules are thin (Charge low is empty); the states have no temper tell by decision (care drafts, Temper and the states); the stage rule and the states have five simulator tests (`prototype/netling2/sides.test.js`), no game tests.
